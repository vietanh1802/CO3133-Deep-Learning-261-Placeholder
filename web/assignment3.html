Dataset and EDA

Flickr8k pairs 8,091 everyday photographs with five English captions each, all written for that photograph. Image-text retrieval asks a model to find the matching captions for an image, and the matching image for a caption.

Data quality and splits

Every image decodes and has exactly five captions, and every caption belongs to an existing image, so no modality is missing. Five caption lines name an image that is not in the archive, and we drop them. We keep the official split and leave out the 91 images that appear in no split file.

The split unit is one image with all five of its captions, and no image is listed in two splits. So no test image or test pair is seen during training or model selection. We choose models and checkpoints on the dev split and evaluate the test split once, after all configurations are fixed. A perceptual-hash search followed by a pixel comparison found one pair of identical images, both in the training split, so no duplicate crosses splits.

Two weaker forms of overlap remain. 62 of the 5,000 test captions have exactly the same wording as a training caption, such as "two dogs playing in the snow", although they describe different images. The duplicate check also cannot find different photographs of the same event, because the release has no photographer or event identifiers.

Captions and images

Training captions have 2 to 36 tokens, with a median of 10, and the three splits have nearly the same length distribution. Dogs and people dominate the content: "dog" occurs 6,159 times and "man" 5,374 times in the 30,000 training captions. Only 1.22% of test tokens never occur in the training captions.

Images span 597 sizes, and their long side is at most 500 pixels. Only 48 of 8,091 images have a short side below 224 pixels, the usual input size of pretrained image encoders.

Pair quality

The release includes human judgements of image-caption pairs on the 1,000 test images. Crowd workers confirmed 700 of the 721 captions they judged against their own image, and the 21 others are kept for inspection during error analysis.

The judgements also show a weakness of the retrieval metric, which accepts only the captions written for an image. Three experts all gave the top score to 103 of 5,664 captions written for another image. In addition, 7 test captions are identical for two or more images. Recall@K therefore slightly underestimates true retrieval quality.
