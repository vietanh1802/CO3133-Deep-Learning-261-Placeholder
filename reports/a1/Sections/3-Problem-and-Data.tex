\section{Problem and data description}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Task and research question}
We classify each image into one of ten clothing categories. This task can help
organize online product catalogs. We compare the models on the same data splits
to identify differences in accuracy and common errors.

For Fashion-MNIST, the model receives a normalized grayscale tensor
$x\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times28\times28}$ and returns ten class scores
$f_\theta(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{10}$. Its prediction is
$\hat y=\arg\max_{k\in\{0,\ldots,9\}} f_\theta(x)_k$.
Low resolution and similar garment outlines
make some categories difficult to separate.

\subsection{Dataset and study scope}
Fashion-MNIST contains 70,000 Zalando product images with supplied category
labels: 60,000 official training images and 10,000 official test images
\cite{fashionmnist}. Figure~\ref{fig:data-samples} shows the ten categories.

We use the authors' MIT-licensed dataset in compressed IDX format (30.88 MB)
\cite{fashionmnist-data}. These results apply to small product images from ten
known categories. We do not evaluate full scenes or unseen categories.

Fashion-MNIST is the main comparison dataset. MNIST handwritten digits are used
for development, and CIFAR-10 colour images provide a separate extension
\cite{mnist-data,cifar10-data}. Table~\ref{tab:data-splits} lists the dataset splits.

\subsection{Splits and leakage prevention}
\label{sec:splits}
For each dataset, a random permutation with seed 42 reserves 10\% of the official
training set for validation. The split unit is one image, and sampling is not
stratified. For Fashion-MNIST, class counts therefore range from 5,353 to 5,445
in training and from 555 to 647 in validation. All models use the same indices
for a given dataset. Using identical splits ensures that models do not receive different training
or validation images. The small differences in class counts come from random
sampling rather than class-based selection.

We use validation loss to select checkpoints, while the official test sets are
reserved for final evaluation. Although training and validation indices do not
overlap, we have not checked for duplicate images or images of the same product
across splits.

\subsection{Preprocessing and augmentation}
\label{sec:preprocessing}
MNIST and Fashion-MNIST keep their native resolution. Pixels are converted to
float32 in $[0,1]$, then normalized as $x'=(x-0.5)/0.5$. Training adds random
rotation within $\pm10^\circ$ and translation up to 10\% of image size. These transformations vary the position and orientation of a product without
changing its target label, reducing reliance on its exact pixel location.
Normalization maps the original intensity range to $[-1,1]$.

CIFAR-10 uses four-pixel padding, a $32\times32$ random crop, and a random
horizontal flip for training. We normalize the red, green, and blue channels using the fixed means
$(0.4914,0.4822,0.4465)$ and standard deviations $(0.2470,0.2435,0.2616)$.
These are channel statistics of the original 50,000-image CIFAR-10 training
set after scaling pixels to $[0,1]$. For each channel, we subtract its mean and
divide by its standard deviation to centre and rescale the input. Validation
and test images use the same constants without random augmentation.

For example, a Fashion-MNIST validation batch has 128 grayscale images of
$28\times28$ pixels, with one class label per image.
